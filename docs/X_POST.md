## Draft A: thread (recommended)

1/ The borehole at Ugbo was drilled with a donor's money. A ribbon, a plaque, a photo.
Two years later the pump's seal wore out. A mechanic would fix it for a few bags of cement. There was no fund. The women went back to the stream.
(Ugbo is made up. The problem is not.)

2/ About 1 in 4 handpumps in sub-Saharan Africa is out of order at any time. The money that builds a well is almost never the money that keeps it running.
Who pays for the repair? And how does a donor know it was spent on the pump?

3/ I built WellKeep at #NEARHackathon with @NEARProtocol.
Every water point is an NFT and its own account that holds its repair money in USDC. Nobody holds a key to it. A donor can check that in the browser before giving a cent.

4/ A household reports a broken pump with a one-time code from a printed card. A mechanic quotes. After the fix, the reporter and about 8 random households are asked: "does it work now?"
Two thirds yes, and the mechanic is paid from the pump's own account. 70% now, 30% an hour later.

5/ Every report, vote and payment is public, with an invoice and a CSV for the bookkeeper. A village committee (a real Sputnik DAO) approves the big quotes. Mechanics earn reputation from finished jobs.

6/ Honest limits: it's a testnet prototype with test USDC, a made-up village and no field test yet. I'd love to hear from water charities and funders who could tell me where this breaks in a real village.
Live: https://wellkeep.ibnweb3lab.workers.dev
Code: https://github.com/ibnweb3/wellkeep
